Temperature scales

A temperature scale is a set of numbers that measure how hot or cold something is. Three are in common use: Celsius (°C), Fahrenheit (°F) and Kelvin (K). They measure the same thing, so any temperature can be written on each of them.

The three scales

  • Celsius sets water's freezing point at 0 and its boiling point at 100, at normal sea-level pressure.
  • Fahrenheit sets water's freezing point at 32 and its boiling point at 212. One Fahrenheit degree is 5/9 of a Celsius degree.
  • Kelvin uses the same size of degree as Celsius, but starts at absolute zero, the lowest possible temperature. So 0 K is the coldest possible, and water freezes at 273.15 K.

The conversion formulas

From Celsius to Fahrenheit: multiply by 9, divide by 5, then add 32. In short, F = C × 9/5 + 32.

From Fahrenheit to Celsius: subtract 32, multiply by 5, then divide by 9. In short, C = (F − 32) × 5/9.

From Celsius to Kelvin: add 273.15. In short, K = C + 273.15. Kelvin is never negative, so the formula only ever gives a number of zero or more.

Worked examples

20 °C to Fahrenheit. 20 × 9 = 180. 180 ÷ 5 = 36. 36 + 32 = 68. So 20 °C is 68 °F.

98 °F to Celsius. 98 − 32 = 66. 66 × 5 = 330. 330 ÷ 9 = 36.67 (rounded). So 98 °F is about 36.7 °C.

300 K to Celsius. 300 − 273.15 = 26.85. So 300 K is 26.85 °C. To get Fahrenheit as well: 26.85 × 9 = 241.65, ÷ 5 = 48.33, + 32 = 80.33. So it is about 80.3 °F.

−40 is the same on both. Check it: −40 × 9 = −360, ÷ 5 = −72, + 32 = −40. That is the one point where the two scales meet.

Try it

What is 0 °C in Fahrenheit? (Answer: 0 × 9/5 + 32 = 32 °F.) What is 25 °C in Kelvin? (Answer: 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K.)
