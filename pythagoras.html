The Pythagorean theorem explained

The Pythagorean theorem is a rule about right-angled triangles. A right angle is a corner that looks like the corner of a square, and it is marked as 90 degrees. If you know two sides of a right-angled triangle, the rule tells you the third.

The parts of a right-angled triangle

  • The hypotenuse is the longest side. It is always the side opposite the right angle. Call it c.
  • The legs are the two shorter sides that meet at the right angle. Call them a and b.

The rule

a² + b² = c²

Here a² means a × a, and the same goes for b² and c². In words: square each leg, add the two results, and you get the square of the hypotenuse.

Checking the rule with a 3, 4, 5 triangle

Take a triangle with legs 3 and 4.

  1. Square the legs: 3 × 3 = 9 and 4 × 4 = 16.
  2. Add them: 9 + 16 = 25.
  3. So c² = 25. The hypotenuse is the square root of 25, which is 5.

The sides are 3, 4 and 5. The rule holds.

Finding the hypotenuse

When both legs are known, square each one, add the results, then take the square root.

Worked example: legs 6 and 8.

  1. 6 × 6 = 36 and 8 × 8 = 64.
  2. 36 + 64 = 100.
  3. c = √100 = 10. The hypotenuse is 10.

Finding a missing leg

When the hypotenuse and one leg are known, the rule runs backwards. Square both known sides, subtract the smaller square from the larger, then take the square root.

Worked example: hypotenuse 13 and leg 5.

  1. 13 × 13 = 169 and 5 × 5 = 25.
  2. 169 − 25 = 144.
  3. b = √144 = 12. The other leg is 12.

Always subtract the smaller square from the larger one. The hypotenuse is the longest side, so its square is always the biggest.

A hypotenuse that is not a whole number

Legs 2 and 3 give c² = 4 + 9 = 13. Since 13 is not a perfect square, c = √13 is not a whole number. Its value is about 3.61. To check, 3.61 × 3.61 = 13.03 is close to 13, so the answer is a sensible approximation. Square roots like this are explained on the square roots page.

The 6, 8, 10 triangle is the 3, 4, 5 triangle with every side doubled. Multiplying all three sides by the same number always gives another right-angled triangle.

Remember the order

Work out the squares first, then the addition or subtraction, then the square root last. This is the same order as in the order of operations page: powers come before adding and subtracting.

Try it

  1. A right-angled triangle has legs 9 and 12. What is the hypotenuse? (Answer: 15, since 81 + 144 = 225.)
  2. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 10 and leg 6. What is the other leg? (Answer: 8, since 100 − 36 = 64.)
