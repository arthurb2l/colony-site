Inequalities: less than, greater than, and number lines

An equation says two things are equal, such as 2 + 3 = 5. An inequality says one thing is bigger or smaller than another. It uses four signs. This page explains what each sign means, how to read one on a number line, and how to check a solution.

The four signs

SignNameReads asExample
<less thanthe left side is smaller3 < 7
>greater thanthe left side is bigger7 > 3
≤less than or equal tosmaller, or the same5 ≤ 5 is true
≥greater than or equal tobigger, or the same5 ≥ 5 is true

A simple way to remember the direction: the wide end of the sign faces the bigger number. In 3 < 7 the wide end is on the 7, and in 7 > 3 the wide end is on the 7 too.

Reading inequalities on a number line

A number line puts smaller numbers on the left and bigger numbers on the right. So any number that is further right is greater.

  • x < 4 means every number to the left of 4. Draw an open circle at 4, because 4 itself is not included, and shade to the left.
  • x ≥ −1 means −1 and every number to the right of it. Draw a filled dot at −1, because −1 is included, and shade to the right.

The open circle and the filled dot are the only difference between a strict sign (< or >) and a sign with "or equal to" (≤ or ≥).

Negative numbers

On a number line, −9 is to the left of −2, so −9 < −2 is true. Many people expect the opposite because 9 is bigger than 2. The rule is about position on the line, not the size of the digits. For more on this, see negative numbers.

Checking a solution

Take the inequality x < 5. Which numbers make it true? Try a few:

Try x =Is x < 5?
3Yes, 3 < 5
5No, 5 is not less than 5
6No, 6 is not less than 5
−10Yes, −10 < 5

So the solutions are all numbers below 5, and 5 itself is not one of them. This matches the open circle at 5 on the number line.

To check your answer, pick one value inside your solution and one value outside it. The inside value should make the inequality true and the outside value should make it false. If not, the line or the direction of the sign is wrong.

Solving a simple inequality

Solve x + 2 < 9. Subtract 2 from both sides, the same as you would for an equation:

x + 2 − 2 < 9 − 2, so x < 7.

Check the answer: x = 6 gives 6 + 2 = 8, and 8 < 9 is true. x = 8 gives 10, and 10 < 9 is false. Both checks agree with x < 7.

Try it

  1. Which is bigger, 12 or −12?
  2. Is −3 ≥ −3 true or false?
  3. Which whole numbers fit x ≤ 2 when x is at least 0?

Answers: 12 (it is further right); true (equal counts for ≥); 0, 1 and 2.
