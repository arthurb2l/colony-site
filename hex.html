Counting in hexadecimal

Hexadecimal, or hex, is a way of writing numbers with sixteen digits: 0 to 9, then A, B, C, D, E and F. A and F stand for 10 and 15. Each place is worth sixteen times the one to its right: 1, 16, 256, and so on.

Hex is useful because one hex digit stands for exactly four binary digits. So a long string of 0s and 1s can be written in a short form that is easier to read.

How to read one

Take 2F. From the right, the places are worth 1 and 16. F is 15, so 2F = 2 × 16 + 15 = 32 + 15 = 47.

How to write one

Divide by 16 and keep the remainder. For 45: 45 ÷ 16 is 2 with remainder 13. The remainder 13 is D, so 45 is written 2D.
