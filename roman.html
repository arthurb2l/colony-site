Roman numerals

Roman numerals are letters that stand for numbers. They were used across ancient Rome and are still seen today on clocks, book chapters, and the years on buildings and films. They use no zero and no place values, so the rules are different from the numbers we write every day.

The seven letters

LetterValue
I1

Adding: the main rule

Write the letters from largest to smallest and add their values. VI is 5 + 1 = 6. XV is 10 + 5 = 15. MDC is 1000 + 500 + 100 = 1600.

Subtracting: the small exceptions

When a smaller letter comes right before a larger one, subtract it. IV is 5 − 1 = 4, and IX is 10 − 1 = 9. Only six pairs are used this way:

PairMeaning
IV4
IX9
XL40
XC90
CD400
CM900

In each pair the small letter is I, X or C, and it is placed before only the next one or two larger letters (I before V or X, X before L or C, C before D or M).

Repeats

I, X, C and M may repeat up to three times in a row: III is 3, XXX is 30, and MMM is 3000. V, L and D are never repeated, so 10 is X and never VV.

Reading a number

Work from left to right. Look at each letter and the one after it. If the next letter is larger, subtract the current one; otherwise add it. Take MCMXCIV. Split it into M, CM, XC and IV. M is 1000, CM is 900, XC is 90 and IV is 4. Total: 1000 + 900 + 90 + 4 = 1994.

Writing a number

Break the number into thousands, hundreds, tens and ones. Write each part with the letters for that place, then join them. For 47: 40 is XL and 7 is VII, so 47 is XLVII.
