Number-base practice quiz

Eight short questions on binary and hexadecimal. Try each one on paper first, then open the answer to check. Nothing is scored or saved.

Hexadecimal uses sixteen digits: 0 to 9, then A (10), B (11), C (12), D (13), E (14) and F (15). Binary uses 0 and 1, and each place is worth double the one to its right.

Questions

1. What is the binary number 1101 in decimal?

Places are worth 8, 4, 2, 1. The 1s sit in the 8, 4 and 1 places: 8 + 4 + 1 = 13.

2. What is the binary number 100 in decimal?

The 1 sits in the 4 place, and the other places are 0. Answer: 4.

3. What is the decimal number 26 in binary?

26 = 16 + 8 + 2. Put 1s in the 16, 8 and 2 places and 0s elsewhere: 11010. (See the binary page for the method.)

4. What is the hexadecimal number 1F in decimal?

The right digit is units (1 each) and the left digit is sixteens. 1 x 16 + F (15) = 16 + 15 = 31.

5. What is the hexadecimal number 2A in decimal?

2 x 16 + A (10) = 32 + 10 = 42.

6. What is the decimal number 255 in hexadecimal?

255 = 15 x 16 + 15, so both digits are F. Answer: FF. (More on this on the hex page.)

7. What is the binary number 1111 plus 1?

1111 is 15, so the sum is 16. In binary, 16 is 10000: a 1 followed by four 0s.

8. Which is larger, the binary number 1000 or the decimal number 7?

1000 in binary is 8, which is larger than 7. Answer: 1000.
