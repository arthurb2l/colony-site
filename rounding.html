Rounding explained

Rounding replaces a number with a nearby number that is simpler to say or use. Ninety-seven people is "about a hundred", and 3.14159 is "about 3.14". This page shows the one rule behind rounding, how to apply it to any place value, and where rounding can go wrong.

The rule

Choose the place you want to round to, such as tens, hundreds or tenths. Look at the digit just to the right of that place:

  • If it is 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, round down: keep the digit in your place and replace everything to its right with zeros (or drop it, after a decimal point).
  • If it is 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9, round up: add 1 to the digit in your place, then replace everything to its right with zeros (or drop it).

The digit 5 is the boundary case. This page rounds halves up, which is the common school convention: 45 rounded to the nearest ten is 50.

Check 1,249 by place value: it is 1 thousand, 2 hundreds, 4 tens and 9 ones. The tens digit is 4, so the hundreds stay at 2 and the rest become zeros: 1,200.

Rounding decimals

The same rule works after the decimal point. To round to two decimal places, look at the third decimal digit.

  • 3.14159 to two decimal places: the third decimal digit is 1, so the result is 3.14.
  • 6.25 to one decimal place: the second decimal digit is 5, so round up to 6.3.
  • 0.149 to two decimal places: the third decimal digit is 9, so round up from 0.14 to 0.15.

Why round?

Rounding makes numbers easy to compare and to estimate with. If a town's library lends 4,768 books in a year, "about 5,000" tells you the scale at a glance, and mental arithmetic with 5,000 is much quicker than with 4,768.

Where rounding goes wrong

Rounding loses information, so do it at the end of a calculation, not in the middle. Suppose you round each number before adding:

  • 2.4 rounds to 2, and 2.4 rounds to 2, so 2 + 2 = 4.
  • The exact sum is 2.4 + 2.4 = 4.8, which rounds to 5.

Rounding early gave 4, but the correct answer to the nearest whole number is 5. Keep the full values until the last step.

Try it

  1. Round 862 to the nearest ten.
  2. Round 865 to the nearest ten.
  3. Round 1,963 to the nearest hundred.
  4. Round 7.45 to one decimal place.
  5. Round 0.149 to two decimal places.
Answers

1. 860 (the digit 2 is down). 2. 870 (the digit 5 is up). 3. 2,000 (the tens digit 6 is up, so 19 hundreds become 20 hundreds). 4. 7.5 (the second decimal digit 5 is up). 5. 0.15 (the third decimal digit 9 is up, so 0.14 becomes 0.15).
