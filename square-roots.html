Square roots explained

A square root reverses squaring. Squaring a number means multiplying it by itself: 6 squared is 6 × 6 = 36. The square root goes the other way: the square root of 36 is 6. The symbol for square root is √, so √36 = 6.

Squaring and rooting are opposites

Start with any whole number, square it, then take the square root of the result. You get the number back.

  • Square 7: 7 × 7 = 49. Then √49 = 7.
  • Square 12: 12 × 12 = 144. Then √144 = 12.

Every positive number has exactly one positive square root. This page uses the positive root, so √49 = 7, not −7, even though (−7) × (−7) is also 49.

Perfect squares

A perfect square is a whole number that is the square of another whole number. The first twelve are worth knowing by heart, because they make root questions quick.

Reading the table backwards gives square roots. For example, 81 is in the second row, above 9, so √81 = 9.

Estimating a root that is not a perfect square

Many numbers are not perfect squares, such as 50 or 20. You can still place their square roots between two whole numbers.

  1. Find the perfect squares just below and just above the number.
  2. Take the square roots of those two squares. The root of your number lies between them.

For 50: 49 is just below 50 and 64 is just above it. Since √49 = 7 and √64 = 8, the value of √50 is between 7 and 8. Because 50 is much closer to 49 than to 64, √50 is a little more than 7.

Simplifying a square root

A root can often be written more simply by pulling out a perfect square factor. The rule is that √(a × b) = √a × √b whenever a and b are not negative.

Worked example: simplify √72.

  1. Find a perfect square that divides 72. The largest one is 36, because 72 = 36 × 2.
  2. Split the root: √72 = √36 × √2.
  3. Take the root of the perfect square: √36 = 6. So √72 = 6√2.

The answer 6√2 is the simplest form. Its approximate value is about 8.49, which lies between 8 and 9, as the estimating method above predicts.

Why it matters

Square roots show up when you find the side of a square from its area. A square with an area of 49 square units has sides of √49 = 7 units. They also appear in the Pythagorean theorem, which you will see once you study right-angled triangles.

Quick check

  1. What is √81?
  2. Between which two whole numbers does √50 lie?
  3. Simplify √32.
  4. What is √144?
  5. Simplify √20.
Answers
  1. 9, because 9 × 9 = 81.
  2. Between 7 and 8, because 49 is below 50 and 64 is above it.
  3. 4√2. Since 32 = 16 × 2, √32 = √16 × √2 = 4√2.
  4. 12, because 12 × 12 = 144.
  5. 2√5. Since 20 = 4 × 5, √20 = √4 × √5 = 2√5.
