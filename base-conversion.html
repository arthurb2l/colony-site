Converting between number bases

A number can be written in any base. To convert a whole number from everyday (decimal) counting into another base, repeatedly divide by the new base and keep the remainders. This works for binary (base 2), octal (base 8), hex (base 16), and any other base.

The method

  1. Divide the number by the new base. Write down the remainder.
  2. Replace the number with the quotient (the whole-number result of the division).
  3. Repeat until the quotient is 0.
  4. Read the remainders from the last one to the first. That is the answer.

Example 1: 13 in binary

DivideQuotientRemainder
13 ÷ 261
6 ÷ 230
3 ÷ 211
1 ÷ 201

Read the remainders from the bottom up: 1101. Check: 8 + 4 + 1 = 13.

Example 2: 64 in octal

DivideQuotientRemainder
64 ÷ 880
8 ÷ 810
1 ÷ 801

Read the remainders from the bottom up: 100. Check: 1 × 64 = 64.

Example 3: 255 in hex

DivideQuotientRemainder
255 ÷ 161515 (F)
15 ÷ 16015 (F)

Read the remainders from the bottom up: F, F, so the answer is FF. Check: 15 × 16 + 15 = 255. In hex, the remainders 10 to 15 are written A to F.

Why it works

Each remainder is the digit for one place. The first remainder is the ones place, the second is the base place, the third is the base-squared place, and so on. Reading from the bottom up puts the largest place first, which is the usual order for writing a number.

Going the other way

To convert back, multiply each digit by its place value and add. For 1101 in binary: 1 × 8 + 1 × 4 + 0 × 2 + 1 × 1 = 13.

Practice questions

  1. Convert 6 to binary.
  2. Convert 20 to octal.
  3. Convert 26 to hex.

Answers

  1. 110. Check: 4 + 2 = 6.
  2. 24. Check: 2 × 8 + 4 = 20.
  3. 1A. Check: 1 × 16 + 10 = 26.
