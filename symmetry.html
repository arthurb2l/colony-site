Symmetry explained

A shape has symmetry when part of it is a mirror image of another part, or when it can be turned and still look the same. There are two kinds to learn: lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry.

Lines of symmetry

A line of symmetry is a line you could fold a shape along so that the two halves match exactly. Picture a mirror placed on the line: the reflection completes the shape.

A rectangle that is not a square has two lines of symmetry, one across the middle and one down the middle. The dashed line below is one of them.

Symmetry in letters

Capital letters are a good everyday test. The letter A has one line of symmetry, straight down the middle. The letter H has two, one vertical and one horizontal. The letter F has none, because its top bar sits on one side only. Check each letter by asking whether the left half matches the right half when folded.

Rotational symmetry

A shape has rotational symmetry if it looks the same after a turn of less than a full circle. The order is how many times it matches itself in one full turn of 360°. A shape that only matches after a complete turn has order 1, which means no rotational symmetry.

Worked check for a square: turn it 90° and it looks the same. Turns of 90°, 180° and 270° also give matches, and the fourth turn returns to the start. That is 4 matches in one full turn, so the order is 4.

The number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry are not always the same. A square has 4 of each and an equilateral triangle has 3 of each. A rectangle that is not a square has 2 lines and order 2. A parallelogram that is not a rectangle has no lines of symmetry but still has order 2.

Check your understanding

  1. How many lines of symmetry does a regular hexagon have? (Answer: 6.)
  2. What is the order of rotational symmetry of an equilateral triangle? (Answer: 3, since it matches every 120°.)
  3. Does the letter F have a line of symmetry? (Answer: no.)
  4. A shape looks the same after a 180° turn but not after a 90° turn. What is its order? (Answer: 2.)
