Area and perimeter explained

Area and perimeter both describe a flat shape, but they measure different things. Perimeter is the distance around the outside of a shape. Area is how much flat space the shape covers. A rug that is 5 metres long and 3 metres wide has a perimeter of 16 metres, which is the length of edging needed to go around it, and an area of 15 square metres, which is how much floor it covers.

Rectangles

For a rectangle with length l and width w:

  • Area = length × width = l × w
  • Perimeter = 2 × (length + width) = 2 × (l + w)

Example: a rectangle 5 cm long and 3 cm wide. The area is 5 × 3 = 15 square centimetres. The perimeter is 2 × (5 + 3) = 2 × 8 = 16 centimetres. Check the perimeter by adding the four sides: 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16.

Area is written in square units (cm², m², km²) because it counts squares that fit inside the shape. Perimeter uses plain units (cm, m, km) because it is a length.

A square is a rectangle with four equal sides. If each side is 4 cm, the area is 4 × 4 = 16 square centimetres and the perimeter is 4 × 4 = 16 centimetres. The two numbers happen to match here, but they are still different measures with different units.

Triangles

A triangle has a base and a height. The height is measured straight up from the base to the opposite corner, at a right angle to the base. For a triangle with base b and height h:

  • Area = ½ × base × height

Example: base 6 cm, height 4 cm. The area is ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 square centimetres. Think of the triangle as half of a rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm, which has area 24.

The perimeter of a triangle is the sum of its three sides. Unlike the area, it needs all three side lengths, not just the base and height.

Parallelograms

A parallelogram is a four-sided shape with two pairs of parallel sides. Its area is base × height, where the height is measured at a right angle to the base, not along the slanted side.

Example: base 7 cm, height 3 cm. The area is 7 × 3 = 21 square centimetres.

Common mistakes

  • Using the slanted side instead of the height when finding a triangle or parallelogram area.
  • Forgetting the ½ for a triangle.
  • Adding only two sides of a rectangle for the perimeter. Remember there are four sides.
  • Writing the answer without units, or using units like cm for an area.

Try it

  1. A rectangle is 9 cm long and 2 cm wide. Find the area and the perimeter.
  2. A square has sides of 7 cm. Find the area and the perimeter.
  3. A triangle has base 10 cm and height 3 cm. Find the area.
  4. A rectangle has an area of 24 square cm and sides of 4 cm and 6 cm. Find its perimeter.
Answers

1. Area 18 square cm (9 × 2). Perimeter 22 cm (2 × (9 + 2)).
2. Area 49 square cm (7 × 7). Perimeter 28 cm (4 × 7).
3. Area 15 square cm (½ × 10 × 3).
4. Perimeter 20 cm (2 × (4 + 6)). The area is 4 × 6 = 24, which matches.
