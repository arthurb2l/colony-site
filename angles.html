Angles explained

An angle measures how far one line turns away from another at a shared point, called the vertex. Angles are measured in degrees, written with a small circle: 90° is a quarter turn. This page explains how angles are measured, how they are named by size, and how angles add up on a straight line and around a point. It ends with a quick check.

Measuring in degrees

A full turn is 360°. Half a turn is 180°, and a quarter turn is 90°. A protractor is a half-circle tool marked from 0° to 180°: put its centre on the vertex, line up one arm of the angle with the 0° line, and read the mark where the other arm crosses the scale.

Read the scale that starts at the arm you are measuring from. Many protractors have two scales, one running each way, so pick the one that starts at 0° on the arm you chose.

Naming angles by size

TypeSizeExample
Acuteless than 90°a slice of pizza, 30°
Rightexactly 90°the corner of a sheet of paper
Obtusemore than 90° and less than 180°a reclining chair, 120°
Straightexactly 180°a straight line seen as an angle
Reflexmore than 180° and less than 360°the long way round a corner, 270°

A useful way to remember the first three: an acute angle is "a little", a right angle is a corner, and an obtuse angle is "a bit more than a corner".

Angles on a straight line

A straight line makes an angle of 180° at any point on it. If a line is split into two angles at a point, those two angles add up to 180°. So if one angle is 135°, the other is 180 − 135 = 45°.

Worked example: a line is split into angles of x and 72°. Then x + 72 = 180, so x = 108°.

Angles around a point

Angles that meet at one point and fill the space around it add up to 360°. If four angles meet at a point and three of them are 80°, 100° and 90°, the fourth is 360 − 270 = 90°.

Two lines that cross make four angles at the crossing point. Opposite angles, called vertically opposite angles, are always equal. Next to each other, any two of them add up to 180°.

Adding angles inside a triangle

The three angles inside any triangle add up to 180°. If two of them are 50° and 60°, the third is 180 − 110 = 70°. A triangle with one right angle is called a right-angled triangle; the other two angles then add up to 90°.

Quick check

  1. What is the size of the corner of a square?
  2. Two angles sit on a straight line. One is 135°. How big is the other?
  3. Four angles meet at a point: 80°, 100°, 90° and an unknown angle. What is the unknown angle?
  4. Is an angle of 150° acute, right, obtuse or reflex?
  5. Two angles of a triangle are 50° and 60°. What is the third?

Answers: 1) 90° (a right angle). 2) 45°. 3) 90°. 4) Obtuse. 5) 70°.
