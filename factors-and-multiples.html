Factors and multiples

A factor of a number divides it exactly, with no remainder. A multiple of a number is what you get when you multiply that number by a whole number. These are two ways of saying the same relationship: 3 is a factor of 12, and 12 is a multiple of 3, because 3 × 4 = 12.

Finding factors

Factors come in pairs that multiply to the number. Example: 12 = 1 × 12, 12 = 2 × 6, and 12 = 3 × 4. The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12.

To find them quickly, test whole numbers from 1 up to the square root of the number. Each time one divides evenly, you also get its partner. Example: for 36, test 1 to 6. That gives 1 × 36, 2 × 18, 3 × 12 and 4 × 9. Then 6 × 6 gives the middle factor. The factors of 36 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18 and 36.

Every number greater than 1 has at least two factors: 1 and itself. The number 1 is the exception, since its only factor is itself. A number with exactly those two factors is a prime.

Finding multiples

Multiples of 3 are 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 and so on. You never run out, because you can keep multiplying by the next whole number. The multiples of a number are all the numbers you can reach by counting up in steps of that number.

To check whether one number is a multiple of another, divide and look for a remainder of zero. Example: 45 ÷ 9 = 5 with no remainder, so 45 is a multiple of 9.

Common factors and common multiples

A factor that two numbers share is a common factor. The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. The factors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. The shared ones are 1, 2, 3 and 6. The largest is 6, which is the greatest common divisor.

A multiple that two numbers share is a common multiple. Multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24. Multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24. The shared ones start 12, 24. The smallest is 12, which is the least common multiple.

Prime factors

Breaking a number into prime factors shows all of its factors at once. Example: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3. Each factor of 12 is made from some of these pieces: 2, 3, 2 × 2 = 4, 2 × 3 = 6, 2 × 2 × 3 = 12, and 1 with no pieces.

Quick check

  1. List all the factors of 16. (Answer: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16)
  2. Is 7 a factor of 28? (Answer: yes, because 7 × 4 = 28)
  3. Is 45 a multiple of 9? (Answer: yes, because 9 × 5 = 45)
  4. What is the smallest common multiple of 3 and 5? (Answer: 15)
  5. What is the largest common factor of 8 and 20? (Answer: 4)
