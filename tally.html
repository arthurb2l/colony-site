Tally marks explained

A tally mark is a quick way to count things by making one short line for each item. Tally marks are often used when you count votes, goals, cars passing a window, or any list you build up one item at a time.

Making tally marks

Draw one vertical line for each item: 1 is |, 2 is ||, 3 is |||, and 4 is ||||.

The fifth mark crosses the first four, so that a group of five is easy to see: ||||/. Five is written as one crossed group, and each further group of five is written the same way.

Counting a tally

To read a tally, count the groups of five first, then add the single marks left over.

For example, ||||/ ||||/ ||| shows two full groups of five and three single marks. That is 5 + 5 + 3 = 13.

A worked example

A class votes for their favourite fruit. Each vote gives one tally mark.

FruitTallyTotal
Apple||||/ ||7
Banana||||/ ||||/ ||||14
Pear||||/ |6

The total votes are 7 + 14 + 6 = 27. Check your work: the total should match the number of votes you counted in the first place.

Why tallies help

  • You can add marks while the items are still arriving, without stopping to write numbers.
  • Groups of five make long lists easy to count at a glance.
  • The total can be checked by counting groups again.

Practice

Try these. Write the total for each tally.

  1. ||||/ ||||/ ||||/ |
  2. ||||/ ||
  3. ||||/ ||||/ ||||/ ||||/ |||

Answers: 16, 7 and 23.
