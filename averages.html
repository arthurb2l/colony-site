Averages explained

An average is one number that stands for a whole list. There are three common kinds: the mean, the median and the mode. Each one answers a slightly different question about the list.

Mean

The mean is the total of the numbers divided by how many numbers there are. Add the numbers, then divide by the count.

Take 2, 4 and 9. They add to 15. There are 3 numbers, so the mean is 15 ÷ 3 = 5.

The mean can be a number that is not in the list. For 1, 2 and 6, the total is 9 and the mean is 3. For 1, 2 and 4, the total is 7 and the mean is 7 ÷ 3, which is about 2.33.

Median

The median is the middle number when the list is sorted from smallest to largest. If the list has an odd count, the median is the middle item. If the count is even, the median is the mean of the two middle items.

Odd count: in 3, 8, 5, sort first to get 3, 5, 8. The median is 5.

Even count: take 1, 3, 5 and 7. The two middle items are 3 and 5. Their mean is (3 + 5) ÷ 2 = 4, so the median is 4.

Mode

The mode is the value that appears most often. A list can have no mode if every value appears once, or more than one mode if several values tie.

In 2, 3, 3 and 5, the value 3 appears twice and the others appear once. So the mode is 3.

Which one to use

The mean uses every number, so one very large or very small number can pull it far away from the rest. The median is not moved as much by one extreme value. The mode is useful when the list holds categories, such as the most common shoe size in a class.

Quick check

  1. What is the mean of 10, 20 and 30? (Answer: 20)
  2. What is the median of 4, 9 and 2? (Answer: 4, because the sorted list is 2, 4, 9)
  3. What is the mode of 5, 7, 7 and 8? (Answer: 7)
  4. What is the median of 10, 2, 8 and 4? (Answer: 6, because the middle two are 4 and 8, and (4 + 8) ÷ 2 = 6)
