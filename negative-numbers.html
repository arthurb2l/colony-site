Negative numbers explained

Counting starts at 1 and goes up: 1, 2, 3, and so on. Zero marks the empty count. A negative number is a value below zero. It is written with a minus sign in front, so -3 is three steps below zero.

The number line

Picture a line with zero in the middle. Positive numbers sit to the right of zero, and they get bigger as you move right. Negative numbers sit to the left, and they get smaller as you move left. So -5 is less than -2, even though 5 is bigger than 2. The distance from zero is 5 in one case and 2 in the other, but -5 is further to the left.

Reading a sign

A number and its opposite are the same distance from zero, on opposite sides. The opposite of 4 is -4, and the opposite of -4 is 4. Adding a number and its opposite always gives 0: 4 + (-4) = 0.

Adding a negative

Adding a negative number moves you left. Adding a positive number moves you right. Example: -3 + 5. Start at -3 and move 5 steps right: -2, -1, 0, 1, 2. So -3 + 5 = 2.

Subtracting to reach a negative

When you subtract a bigger number from a smaller one, the answer is negative. Example: 4 - 7. Take 4 steps down to 0, then 3 more steps below zero. So 4 - 7 = -3.

Multiplying signs

Use these rules for the sign of a product:

  • A positive times a positive is positive: 2 x 3 = 6.
  • A positive times a negative is negative: -2 x 3 = -6, because -2 + -2 + -2 = -6.
  • A negative times a negative is positive: -2 x -3 = 6.

The last rule can look odd. A pattern shows why. Multiply -2 by 3, 2, 1, 0, -1, -2 and -3, and the answers go -6, -4, -2, 0, 2, 4, 6. Each step down in the second number adds 2 to the answer. So -2 x -3 = 6.

Quick check

  1. What is -4 + 9? (Answer: 5)
  2. What is 6 - 10? (Answer: -4)
  3. What is -1 x -7? (Answer: 7)
  4. What is -5 x 2? (Answer: -10)
