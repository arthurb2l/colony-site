Long division explained

Long division splits a number into equal groups, step by step. It answers a question such as: how many groups of 6 fit into 347, and what is left over? It uses multiplication and subtraction, one digit at a time.

The words

  • Dividend is the number being divided (347).
  • Divisor is the number of equal groups we make (6).
  • Quotient is the answer, written above the line (57).
  • Remainder is what is left over after the last subtraction (5).

The four steps

Each round repeats the same four steps: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down.

  1. Divide. Find the largest whole number that, times the divisor, does not go past the current part.
  2. Multiply. Multiply that digit by the divisor and write the result under the current part.
  3. Subtract. Take the result away. The answer must be smaller than the divisor.
  4. Bring down. Bring down the next digit of the dividend next to the remainder, and repeat.

Worked example: 347 ÷ 6

  • 6 does not go into 3, so take the first two digits: 34.
  • 6 goes into 34 five times (6 × 5 = 30). Write 5 above the 4. Subtract: 34 − 30 = 4.
  • Bring down the 7 to make 47. 6 goes into 47 seven times (6 × 7 = 42). Write 7 after the 5. Subtract: 47 − 42 = 5.
  • There are no digits left to bring down. The quotient is 57 and the remainder is 5.

Check by multiplying back: 6 × 57 = 342, and 342 + 5 = 347. The check works.

Worked example: 432 ÷ 12

  • 12 does not go into 4, so take 43. 12 goes into 43 three times (12 × 3 = 36). Subtract: 43 − 36 = 7.
  • Bring down the 2 to make 72. 12 goes into 72 six times (12 × 6 = 72). Subtract: 72 − 72 = 0.
  • Nothing is left, so the quotient is 36 with no remainder.

Check by multiplying back: 12 × 36 = 432. The check works.

Worked example: 1058 ÷ 7

  • 7 does not go into 1, so take 10. 7 goes into 10 once (7 × 1 = 7). Subtract: 10 − 7 = 3.
  • Bring down the 5 to make 35. 7 goes into 35 five times. Subtract: 35 − 35 = 0.
  • Bring down the 8 to make 8. 7 goes into 8 once. Subtract: 8 − 7 = 1.
  • The quotient is 151 and the remainder is 1.

Check by multiplying back: 7 × 151 = 1057, and 1057 + 1 = 1058. The check works.

Always check that each subtraction gives a number smaller than the divisor. If it does not, the digit you wrote in the quotient is too small, so raise it by one.

Where a remainder goes

A remainder can be written three ways. For 347 ÷ 6 these all say the same thing:

  • 57 remainder 5
  • 57 r 5
  • 57 and 5/6 (the remainder becomes a fraction over the divisor)

The fraction form links long division to fractions. Each whole step of the quotient is a full group, and the leftover is part of one more group.

Dividing when the number does not split evenly

Sometimes the last remainder is not zero, and you need more digits. Add a decimal point after the last digit of the dividend, then add zeros. Keep going until the remainder is zero or you have enough places. For example, 5 ÷ 4: 4 goes into 5 once with 1 left over. Add a decimal point and a zero to make 10. 4 goes into 10 twice with 2 left over. Add another zero to make 20. 4 goes into 20 five times. So 5 ÷ 4 = 1.25. See decimals for more on place value after the point.

Quick check

Try these yourself, then check the answers below.

  1. 275 ÷ 5
  2. 588 ÷ 4
  3. 203 ÷ 6
  4. 900 ÷ 15
  5. 1000 ÷ 8
Answers
  1. 55 (5 × 55 = 275)
  2. 147 (4 × 147 = 588)
  3. 33 remainder 5 (6 × 33 = 198, and 198 + 5 = 203)
  4. 60 (15 × 60 = 900)
  5. 125 (8 × 125 = 1000), no remainder

Divisibility shortcut

Before you divide, the divisibility rules can tell you whether the remainder will be zero. For example, 432 is divisible by 4 because its last two digits (32) are divisible by 4.

Multiplication is the reverse of division, so it is the best way to check any answer.
