Greatest common divisor and least common multiple

Two numbers can share divisors and multiples. The greatest common divisor (GCD) is the largest number that divides both. The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that both divide into. Both are useful when you add fractions or line up repeating events.

Greatest common divisor

List the divisors of each number and pick the largest one they share. Example: the divisors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. The divisors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. The largest shared divisor is 6, so GCD(12, 18) = 6.

A quicker way for bigger numbers: find a prime factor both numbers share and divide both by it. Repeat until no shared factor is left, then multiply what you divided by. For 24 and 36: both are even, so divide by 2 to get 12 and 18. Both are even again, so divide by 2 to get 6 and 9. Both are divisible by 3, so divide by 3 to get 2 and 3. No shared factor is left. GCD = 2 × 2 × 3 = 12. Check: 24 = 12 × 2 and 36 = 12 × 3.

If the GCD is 1, the numbers have no common divisor besides 1. They are called coprime. Example: 7 and 13 are coprime.

Least common multiple

List the multiples of each number and pick the smallest one they share. Example: multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24. Multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24. The smallest shared one is 12, so LCM(4, 6) = 12.

When you already know the GCD, use this rule: LCM(a, b) = a × b ÷ GCD(a, b). Example: GCD(4, 6) = 2, so LCM = 4 × 6 ÷ 2 = 12.

Why it helps with fractions

To add 1/4 and 1/6, you need a common denominator. The LCM of 4 and 6 is 12, so write 1/4 = 3/12 and 1/6 = 2/12. Their sum is 5/12.

The GCD simplifies fractions. Divide the top and bottom by their GCD. Example: GCD(6, 8) = 2, so 6/8 = 3/4.

Quick check

  1. What is GCD(16, 24)? (Answer: 8)
  2. What is LCM(6, 8)? (Answer: 24)
  3. What is GCD(9, 12)? (Answer: 3)
  4. What is LCM(2, 5)? (Answer: 10, because 2 and 5 are coprime)
