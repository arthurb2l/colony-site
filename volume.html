Volume and capacity explained

Volume is how much space a solid object takes up. Capacity is how much a container can hold. For a box, the two are the same idea: a box that is 10 cm on every side has a volume of 1000 cubic centimetres and can hold 1000 millilitres of water, which is one litre.

Cubic units

Area counts squares that fit on a flat surface. Volume counts cubes that fill a solid. That is why volume is written in cubic units: cm³ (cubic centimetres), m³ (cubic metres) and so on. The little 3 means three directions are multiplied together: length, width and height.

A cube with edges 1 cm long has a volume of 1 cm³. Picture filling a box with these tiny cubes, one layer at a time. The number of cubes you need is the volume.

Volume of a cuboid

A cuboid is a box shape with six rectangular faces. For a cuboid with length l, width w and height h:

Volume = length × width × height = l × w × h

Worked example: a cuboid is 4 cm long, 3 cm wide and 2 cm high.

  1. Multiply the length by the width: 4 × 3 = 12. This is the number of cubes that fit in one layer on the base.
  2. Multiply by the height: 12 × 2 = 24. There are two layers, so 2 × 12 = 24.
  3. The volume is 24 cm³.

The order of the three numbers does not change the answer, because 4 × 3 × 2 = 2 × 4 × 3 = 24. Always check that all three measurements use the same unit before you multiply. A length in metres and a width in centimetres must be converted first.

Capacity: millilitres and litres

Capacity is measured in litres (L) and millilitres (mL). These units are used for liquids and for containers such as bottles, jugs and tanks.

RelationshipMeaning
1 cm³the same as 1 mL
1000 mLthe same as 1 L
1000 cm³the same as 1 L

So a box that holds 1000 cm³ holds exactly one litre. To change litres to millilitres, multiply by 1000. To change millilitres to litres, divide by 1000. For example, 2.5 L = 2.5 × 1000 = 2500 mL, and 750 mL = 750 ÷ 1000 = 0.75 L.

Note: Volume and capacity measure the same kind of space, so the same formula works for both. A container's capacity is usually a little less than its outside volume, because the walls take up space.

Quick check

  1. What is the volume of a cuboid that is 5 cm long, 2 cm wide and 3 cm high?
  2. How many millilitres are in 3 litres?
  3. A box has a volume of 2000 cm³. How many litres can it hold?
Answers
  1. 5 × 2 × 3 = 30 cm³.
  2. 3 × 1000 = 3000 mL.
  3. 2000 ÷ 1000 = 2 L.
