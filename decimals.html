Decimals explained

A decimal is another way to write a number that is not a whole number. It uses a decimal point. The digits to the left of the point are the whole part. The digits to the right of the point are the fractional part. 3.75 has a whole part of 3 and a fractional part of 0.75.

Tenths, hundredths and thousandths

Each digit after the point has a place, just as each digit before the point does. The first place after the point is the tenths place, the second is the hundredths place, and the third is the thousandths place.

So 2.407 means two ones, four tenths, zero hundredths and seven thousandths. Each place is worth one tenth of the place before it.

Decimals as fractions

A decimal with one digit after the point has a denominator of 10. A decimal with two digits has a denominator of 100. A decimal with three digits has a denominator of 1000. Write the digits after the point as the numerator, and use the matching power of ten as the denominator.

0.3 = 3/10. 0.05 = 5/100, which simplifies to 1/20. 0.125 = 125/1000, which simplifies to 1/8.

A fraction can also be written as a decimal. Find an equivalent fraction with a denominator of 10, 100 or 1000, then read off the digits. 3/4 = 75/100 = 0.75. 2/5 = 4/10 = 0.4. For a denominator that does not give a power of ten, divide the top by the bottom. 3/8 = 0.375, because 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.

Comparing decimals

Compare the digits from left to right, the same way you compare whole numbers. Start with the whole part. If those match, compare the tenths, then the hundredths, and so on.

Take 0.4 and 0.35. Both have a whole part of 0. The tenths are 4 and 3, so 0.4 is larger. Adding a zero at the end does not change a decimal, so 0.4 = 0.40, and 0.40 is larger than 0.35.

Take 2.9 and 2.85. The whole parts match at 2. Write the second as 2.90 to line up the places. Then 2.90 is larger than 2.85, so 2.9 is larger.

Adding and subtracting

Line up the decimal points, one under the other, before you add or subtract. Then add or subtract each column as you would with whole numbers. Fill empty places with zeros if that helps.

1.25 + 0.5: write 0.5 as 0.50 so the points line up. 1.25 + 0.50 = 1.75.

3.2 − 1.75: write 3.2 as 3.20. 3.20 − 1.75 = 1.45.

Quick check

  1. Write 0.6 as a fraction in simplest form. (Answer: 3/5)
  2. Which is larger, 0.7 or 0.65? (Answer: 0.7, because 0.70 is larger than 0.65)
  3. Write 1/4 as a decimal. (Answer: 0.25)
  4. Add 2.4 and 0.35. (Answer: 2.75)
