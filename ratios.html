Ratios explained

A ratio compares two or more amounts of the same kind. It is written with a colon, like 3:4, and read as "3 to 4". A recipe that uses 3 cups of flour for every 4 cups of milk has the ratio 3:4. The order matters: 3:4 is not the same as 4:3.

A ratio says how the amounts compare, not how big they are. 3:4 could mean 3 and 4 cups, or 30 and 40 cups, or 3 and 4 paint tins. The same comparison holds in each case.

Equivalent ratios

Multiplying both sides of a ratio by the same number gives an equivalent ratio. 2:3 becomes 4:6 when both sides are multiplied by 2, and 6:9 when both sides are multiplied by 3.

To check whether two ratios match, cross-multiply. 3:4 and 6:8 are equivalent because 3 × 8 = 24 and 4 × 6 = 24. The products are equal, so the ratios are equal. If the products differ, the ratios are different. 3:4 and 5:6 are not equivalent, since 3 × 6 = 18 and 4 × 5 = 20.

Simplifying

A ratio is simplest when the two numbers have no common factor greater than 1. To simplify, divide both sides by their greatest common factor. 12:18 has the common factor 6, so divide both by 6 to get 2:3. Dividing by a common factor works because it is the reverse of the multiplying step above. For a quick check on which numbers divide evenly, see the divisibility rules.

15:25 has the common factor 5, so it simplifies to 3:5. A ratio such as 7:10 cannot be simplified, because 7 and 10 share no factor except 1.

Sharing an amount in a ratio

To split a total in a ratio, first add the parts of the ratio to find the number of equal shares. Then find the value of one share, and multiply it by each part.

Example: share 20 sweets between two friends in the ratio 2:3.

  1. Add the parts: 2 + 3 = 5 shares.
  2. One share is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 sweets.
  3. The first friend gets 2 × 4 = 8 sweets, and the second gets 3 × 4 = 12 sweets.

Check: 8 + 12 = 20, and 8:12 simplifies to 2:3, as required.

A three-part example: share 30 litres of paint in the ratio 1:2:3. The parts add to 6, so one share is 30 ÷ 6 = 5 litres. The amounts are 5, 10 and 15 litres. Check: 5 + 10 + 15 = 30.

Ratio and fraction

A ratio compares one part with another part. A fraction compares one part with the whole. In a bag with 3 red and 4 blue marbles, the ratio of red to blue is 3:4, but red is 3/7 of all the marbles, since the total is 3 + 4 = 7. Both ideas use the same numbers, so it is easy to mix them up. Read the question carefully and check which comparison it asks for. The fractions page covers the whole-based comparison in more detail.

Try it

  • Simplify 8:20. (Answer: 2:5, dividing both by 4.)
  • Is 4:9 equivalent to 12:27? (Yes: 4 × 27 = 108 and 9 × 12 = 108.)
  • Share 45 in the ratio 4:5. (Answer: 20 and 25, because 4 + 5 = 9 and 45 ÷ 9 = 5.)
