Speed, distance and time explained

Speed tells you how far something travels in a given time. Three quantities are linked: distance, time and speed. If you know two of them, you can find the third. This page shows the one rule behind all of them and how to use it with units.

The rule

Speed is distance divided by time:

speed = distance ÷ time

The same rule can be turned around:

  • distance = speed × time
  • time = distance ÷ speed

A triangle helps you remember which one to use. Put distance on top, and speed and time on the bottom, side by side. Cover the quantity you want to find. What is left shows the operation: cover speed and you see distance ÷ time; cover time and you see distance ÷ speed; cover distance and you see speed × time.

A trip example

A bus travels 150 km in 2.5 hours. Its speed is 150 ÷ 2.5 = 60 km/h.

Check: 60 × 2.5 = 150 km. The rule works in both directions.

A cyclist rides at 45 km/h for 3 hours. The distance is 45 × 3 = 135 km.

A car needs to cover 200 km at 80 km/h. The time is 200 ÷ 80 = 2.5 hours, which is 2 hours and 30 minutes. For the part after the decimal point, see decimals, and for the minutes, see time and clocks.

Keeping units consistent

The units must match. If the distance is in kilometres and the time is in hours, the speed is in kilometres per hour (km/h). If the time is in minutes, convert it to hours first, or the answer will be wrong.

Example: a train goes 30 km in 20 minutes. 20 minutes is 20 ÷ 60 = one third of an hour. So the speed is 30 ÷ (1/3) = 30 × 3 = 90 km/h. Check: in 20 minutes at 90 km/h, the train goes 90 ÷ 3 = 30 km. ✓

Converting km/h to m/s

Scientists often use metres per second (m/s). One kilometre is 1,000 metres and one hour is 3,600 seconds. So 1 km/h = 1,000 ÷ 3,600 m/s, which is about 0.2778 m/s. To convert km/h to m/s, divide by 3.6.

Example: 72 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s. Check: 20 m/s × 3.6 = 72 km/h.

To go the other way, multiply by 3.6. For example, 10 m/s × 3.6 = 36 km/h. For more on converting units, see units.

Solving with an equation

You can write the rule as an equation and solve it like any other. For a runner who covers 12 km in 1.5 hours, the speed s is found from s × 1.5 = 12, so s = 12 ÷ 1.5 = 8 km/h. For more on this method, see equations.

Quick check

  1. A car drives 100 km in 4 hours. What is its average speed? (Answer: 25 km/h)
  2. How far does a boat go at 30 km/h in 2.5 hours? (Answer: 75 km)
  3. How long does it take to drive 120 km at 40 km/h? (Answer: 3 hours)
  4. Convert 36 km/h to m/s. (Answer: 10 m/s)
  5. A train goes 30 km in 20 minutes. What is its speed in km/h? (Answer: 90 km/h)
